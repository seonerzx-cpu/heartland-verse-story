Uncle Lim's Poetry Bootcamp: What Makes a Poem a Poem?

📄 Everyday Prose Paragraph (Continuous Sentences) Written margin-to-margin

"The old void deck is quiet at noon. Plastic chairs sit in a neat row under the spinning ceiling fan. Two grandfathers lean over the stone chess table."

📜 Poetic Stanza (4-Line Quatrain with Line Breaks) Arranged into deliberate lines & stanzas

1. The old void deck is quiet at noon,

2. Plastic chairs wait in a neat little row,

3. Beneath the ceiling fan's gentle tune,

4. Where chessmen guard the stone table below.

👴 Uncle Lim's Structural Lesson: Notice how Prose runs continuously from margin to margin in paragraphs, whereas a Poem is sculpted into deliberate Lines and grouped into Stanzas (verses)—such as a 2-line Couplet or a 4-line Quatrain. Poets use Line Breaks to create natural pauses, spotlight key end-words (noon / row / tune / below), and pace how we breathe as we read!

📚 Summary of What You Learned on the Trail

Lv. 11 Poet • 5/5 Riddles Deciphered
1. Personification & Metaphor (Dragon Playground): Giving human actions to non-human things ("mosaic cheeks grin") & direct comparisons ("steel ribs are a rainbow tunnel") in an ABAB Quatrain.
2. Onomatopoeia & Sensory Imagery (Kopitiam): Sound words ("Clink-clank!", "Sizzle-pop!") and sight, smell, and taste imagery that bring a bustling setting to life.
3. Simile & Alliteration (Mama Shop): Comparing with "like/as" ("hang like a rainbow chandelier") and repeated consonant sounds ("Bouncing balls in braided") in AABB Couplets.
4. Persona, Hyperbole & Juxtaposition (Canal & MRT): Writing in a secret first-person Persona ("We") for Inference, exaggerating with Hyperbole, and contrasting Nature vs. Technology (Juxtaposition).
